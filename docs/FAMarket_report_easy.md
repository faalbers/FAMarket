# FAMarket Report — The Easy Version

**What this is:** the same review as `FAMarket_report.md`, but in simple words.
No jargon. With comparisons from daily life.

**The whole report in one sentence:**
Your tool uses very good ingredients, but it never tastes its own cooking.

---

## 1. Think of FAMarket as a fishing net

- The stock market is a huge ocean with ~thousands of fish (stocks).
- A **filter** is a net. It lets small or sick fish swim through,
  and keeps only the fish you want.
- The **parameters** (all those numbers like P/E, ROE, RS Rank) are the
  holes and knots of the net. They decide what gets caught.

The question of the report:
**is this a good net, and does it really catch good fish?**

---

## 2. The good news — your net is well made

Scientists have studied for 30+ years which "net designs" really work.
Your system uses almost all of the proven ones:

**Winners keep winning (momentum).**
- The best-proven rule in all stock research:
  stocks that went up recently often keep going up for a while.
- Like a soccer team on a winning streak — good form tends to continue.
- Your system checks this (RS Rank, price above its average lines,
  price near its yearly high). This is your strongest tool.

**A price near its yearly high is GOOD, not bad.**
- Feels wrong, right? "It's expensive now, I missed it."
- Research says no: stocks at new highs tend to go even higher.
- Like a runner breaking their personal record — they are in top shape,
  not "finished".

**Healthy companies win in the long run (quality).**
- Companies that turn sales into real profit, without heavy debt,
  beat weak companies over time.
- Like buying a house: solid foundation and no hidden cracks matters
  more than a fresh coat of paint.
- Your system measures this well (profit margins, return on capital,
  debt checks).

**When experts raise their forecasts, listen.**
- When many analysts raise their profit estimates for a company,
  the stock often rises in the following weeks.
- Like a restaurant where all the food critics suddenly update their
  reviews from 3 stars to 4 — the crowds come after.
- Your system tracks this (the "Estimates" numbers).

**Companies buying back their own shares.**
- When a company buys its own shares, your slice of the pizza gets
  bigger without you buying more.
- Research: this quietly beats most other "reward the owner" signals.
- Your system catches it (share count going down).

**One special strength: the right ruler for each thing.**
- You cannot measure a fish and a bird with the same ruler.
- A bank, a REIT (real-estate company) and a normal company need
  different measurements. Most tools ignore this. Yours does not —
  it hides the wrong rulers automatically. This is rare and valuable.

## 3. The warnings — where to be careful

**Known tricks lose power once everyone knows them.**
- When a secret shortcut around traffic gets published in the news,
  everyone drives there — and it becomes slow too.
- Research shows: once a stock "trick" is published, more than half of
  its advantage disappears.
- Lesson: expect small edges, not miracles. Use several signals
  together, never just one.

**The squiggly-line indicators (RSI, MACD) are weak alone.**
- These are the zigzag lines traders love on charts.
- Tests show: used alone, they guess about as well as a coin flip.
- Your system only uses them as a "second opinion" — keep it that way.
- Like a thermometer: useful to confirm you are sick, useless to
  predict if you will get sick next month.

**A big dividend is not a quality stamp.**
- A very high dividend often means the price fell (trouble!),
  not that the company is generous.
- Like a shop with "70% OFF EVERYTHING" — sometimes a bargain,
  often a shop that is dying.
- Your system already checks if the dividend is truly affordable
  (paid from real cash) — that check matters more than the size.

**The "forgotten stocks" score (orphan_score) is the weakest bet.**
- The idea: stocks nobody watches can be hidden treasures.
- The evidence for this is old and has gotten weaker.
- Treat it as a place to get ideas — not as proof of a bargain.

---

## 4. The bad news — nobody checks the catch

This is the F grade in the report.

- Your net is well designed. But after every fishing trip,
  **nobody weighs the fish.**
- The system never checks: "the stocks my filter picked 3 months ago —
  did they actually go up?"
- Without that, you only *believe* the filters work. You never *know*.

**The good part: this is easy to fix.**

- Every filter run already saves a dated list of picks.
- A small new tool could look back later and say:
  "your picks from March made +8%, their market group made +3%."
- Like keeping a diary of your fishing trips: after a few months you
  KNOW which net catches the best fish — no guessing.

Why not "backtest" (simulate the past) instead?
- Honest time-travel needs data exactly as it looked back then.
- Your database only knows *today's* numbers. Testing the past with
  today's numbers is like guessing yesterday's weather by looking out
  the window now — it feels right but it lies.
- Watching your real picks go *forward* has no such problem.

---

## 5. What to add, in order of value

**1. The results diary** (described above). Everything else is polish;
this is the proof.

**2. Ranking instead of pass/fail.**
- Today a stock that misses ONE rule by a hair is thrown away.
- Like a job interview where a great candidate is rejected for being
  1 minute late.
- Better (and research agrees): give every stock points and sort them.
  The best total wins. Your system already stores the points
  (the 0-100 "goodness" numbers) — they just are not used this way yet.

**3. The 9-point health check (Piotroski F-Score).**
- A famous simple test: 9 yes/no questions about a company's health.
- Decades of proof behind it, especially for cheap stocks.
- You already have a home-made 7-question version — adding the proven
  9-question original is cheap and easy.

**4. Profit compared to everything the company owns.**
- You measure profit vs sales. The proven version also measures
  profit vs all its property and machines.
- Like judging a farmer not only per harvest, but per acre of land.

**5. Dividend + buyback in ONE number.**
- The proven combo signal. You have both halves — glue them together.

**6. A "growing too fast" warning.**
- Companies that buy and build too much too quickly often disappoint.
- Like a restaurant opening 20 new locations in one year —
  usually it ends with a mess.

---

## 6. Bottom line

- **Your ingredients are good.** Backed by real research,
  better than most hobby tools.
- **Stay humble.** No filter finds only winners.
  Small, steady advantages are the realistic goal.
- **Build the results diary first.** It turns
  "I think my filters work" into "I can see that they work."
